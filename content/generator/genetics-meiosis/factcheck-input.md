### P1 · provenance claim
Meiosis makes eggs and sperm: one round of copying, then two divisions, giving four cells with one copy of each chromosome; human cells have 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, gametes 23; fertilisation restores two copies of each, so the number stays the same from generation to generation
Cites: Wikipedia: Meiosis

### P2 · provenance claim
In prophase I partner chromosomes pair and recombine, with at least one crossover per pair, seen as chiasmata; the random orientation of each pair at metaphase I is the physical basis of independent assortment; in humans maternal DNA recombines about 42 times per meiosis and paternal DNA about 27; female mammals are born with all their oocytes, arrested in prophase I; nondisjunction in oocytes rises with maternal age; silkworm oocytes have no crossovers
Cites: Wikipedia: Meiosis

### P3 · provenance claim
Independent assortment happens at meiotic metaphase I; for human gametes with 23 chromosomes there are 2 to the 23rd, 8,388,608, possible combinations; with crossing over it produces new genetic combinations
Cites: Wikipedia: Mendelian inheritance

### P4 · provenance claim
Weismann (1887, translated 1889; the source beat quotes it word for word): without a reduction the germ-plasm would double each generation, so a nuclear division must halve it; no two germ cells contain the same combination, which is why children of the same parents differ; twins alike in every way come from one egg; nourishment is too feeble to explain the differences
Cites: Weismann, Essays Upon Heredity (1889), Project Gutenberg #48132

### P5 · provenance claim
Walter Sutton studied sperm formation in the grasshopper Brachystola magna and proposed that Mendel’s laws apply to chromosomes (the Boveri–Sutton chromosome theory)
Cites: Wikipedia: Walter Sutton

### P6 · provenance claim

Cites: Wikipedia: Eleanor Carothers

### P7 · provenance claim
